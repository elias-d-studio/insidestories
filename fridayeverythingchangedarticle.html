How a Group of Schoolgirls Changed Social Norms at Their School

When students speak out, powerful change happens

After years of the same routines, one day a brave girl decided to speak out for change. Alma, a student in Miss Ralston's class, helped end stereotypes and sexist social norms.

The school was in a rural area and to get water, it was necessary to go to a pump at the railway station and fill a bucket full of water and bring it back to the school. This job was always given to the boys at the school until Alma decided to ask if the girls could help carry the water, too.

The boys were outraged and upset with this proposition and tried to bully and harass the girls until they gave up their argument, but the girls at the school banded together and stayed strong.

At the end of the week, Miss Ralston decided that the girls would also carry the water bucket. She said, "I believe that the boys felt that what gave them power and importance over the girls was being taken away so they tried to stop it. I had to show them that girls are equal to them and deserved the same chances and privileges." The location of this school in part created this conflict because in a city or a more urban area it would not be necessary to get the water from the pump.
